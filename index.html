The verdict

Type 227664 into xccr.com and a 100-minute server timer starts. That's the whole hook that has kept people guessing since 2004. Here is the short version, each claim backed by data below and in the repo.

Who: a hobbyist in Batu Pahat, Malaysia, living in New Zealand Not Half-Life 2 Not The Lost Experience 2006 puzzle: solved Modern version: abandoned (dead cron, Sep 2024)

Three eras

2004

Domain registered 18 Nov; the GIF glyphs are dated 17 Nov. WHOIS: Batu Pahat, Johor; contact at palmy.net.nz. Half-Life 2 shipped two days before — the name came from the game, not the other way around.

May 2006

A Slashdot signature leaks the code 227664. The Unfiction forum solves a week-long chain of steganographic GIFs (i0–i4), reaching the message “HE IS THE CODE ON SLASHDOT” and the checkpoint: enter 227664, then 974444.

2006–07

The site becomes a grid of 252 “bunkers” you apply for by email. The target image spells HELLO! 227664 in a pixel font — decoded here for the first time (see below).

2008–21

The ASP.NET app rots: dead timer, then a runtime error for years. A 2008 forum post just reads: “Dead.”

Jan 2022

A from-scratch PHP rebuild. A hidden line balance / goal = B1a, a Bitcoin and a Dogecoin address in the page head, the 100-minute timer. This is the live site.

Oct 2026

This teardown: the state model reconstructed, a deposit sent to test it, the key-buffer mechanic found, the QR proven unrecoverable.

The hidden counter — 75 archived homepages

Every counter value ends in …82 (the wallet's 34,682 sat plus 300-sat hourly steps). The goal ends in …64. The timer alone can never land on it — a deposit is structurally required. The counter rises ~300 sat/hour while the timer is alive, decays when it isn't, floors at the wallet balance, and has not moved since Sep 2024. Hover the points.

The experiment: we put money in

15,382 sat

Exactly the gap to the goal (~$12.50), sent to the doorB address on 2026-10-17. Confirmed on-chain. Then the timer was held alive for 24 h and the page snapshotted every 10 min.

no change

The Wayback series explains why: in the B1a phase the wallet held 34,682 sat for 11 months while the counter read 0. The B1b floor equals the balance at the moment of the 2023 slot transition. The wallet is read once, when the author manually starts a slot — not live. A clean negative result.

The keys spell a word, one letter per solved slot

The four keyB rows are a 52-character buffer. Solving the first slot (B1a) overwrote position 1 from a to D. Each future slot overwrites the next character. Read in order, they spell a message. We have one letter.

Green = revealed, amber = the slot currently in play (frozen), grey = locked. The “wallet address” is doubling as the buffer being overwritten — a mechanic undocumented anywhere else in 20 years.

The QR code: real, cropped on purpose, unrecoverable

format infovalid
error correctionH (highest)
mask pattern6
captured~1/3 of symbol
verdictunrecoverable

The format bits decode cleanly, so this is a genuine QR the author cropped to the top-left. But QR data is written bottom-right → up, so the message header sits in the missing corner; what's here is the padding tail. ECC-H corrects ≤22 erased blocks; ~2× that is missing. An information wall, not a tooling gap. Hover modules.
